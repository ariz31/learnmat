Differential Leveling

Transfer elevation from a known benchmark to a second benchmark by a sequence of backsights and foresights taken with an automatic level and a levelling staff.

Field Use Case

Establish vertical control

Differential leveling is used when elevations must be carried accurately across terrain to set grades, drainage lines, pavements, and construction reference points.

Equipment

Automatic level + staff

The instrument provides a horizontal line of sight while a graduated staff supplies the reading from which height of instrument and point elevation are computed.

Outputs

Reduced levels and checks

The field book shows BS and FS readings, reduced levels of turning points, arithmetic check, and if both end benchmarks are known, the closure error and adjustment.

Concept

Carry elevation through temporary points.

A backsight on a known point establishes the height of instrument. A foresight on a turning point transfers elevation forward. The instrument is then moved, the turning point becomes the new reference, and the process repeats until the closing benchmark is reached.

1 · Known control

Start from BM-A

Use the known elevation of the first benchmark as the starting reduced level.

2 · Transfer

Use turning points

Stable turning points temporarily hold the transferred elevation while the instrument is moved ahead.

3 · Close

Check at BM-B

Compare the computed elevation of the closing benchmark with its known value to evaluate closure.

Required formulas
HI = RL + BSHeight of instrument equals the reduced level of the known point plus the backsight reading.
RL = HI − FSReduced level of the forward point equals height of instrument minus the foresight reading.
ΣBS − ΣFS = RLlast − RLfirstArithmetic check for the differential leveling run.
e = RLcomputed − RLknownClosure error at the final benchmark. If accepted, distribute the correction along the run.
Field procedure

Observe in the correct order.

1 · Setup

Level the instrument

Position the automatic level where both the backsight point and the foresight point are visible and balance the line of sight.

2 · Backsight

Establish HI

Read the staff on the known point or previous turning point, then compute the height of instrument.

3 · Foresight

Transfer RL

Read the staff on the turning point or closing benchmark, then reduce its level before moving the instrument again.

Reduction and checks

Field book, arithmetic check, and adjustment.

Arithmetic check

Verify the run

After the last foresight, check that the difference of summed backsights and foresights equals the difference between final and initial reduced levels.

Closure

Compare against known BM-B

If the closing benchmark is known, compute the misclosure, judge whether it is acceptable, and distribute the correction uniformly by setup or proportionally by distance.

Tabulation

Preserve every sight

The table keeps the sequential field record: setup, point, sight type, reading, HI, raw reduced level, correction, and adjusted reduced level.
