FW04 · 1 / 6 · Introduction — Field Use Case

Profile Leveling & Longitudinal Profile

A profile-leveling run is used to determine elevations along a selected centerline so the ground surface can be reduced, checked for closure, and presented as a longitudinal engineering profile.

Profile corridor

0+000 to 0+100

Principal profile stations are observed every 20 m along the centerline.

Vertical control

BM-A → TP-A → BM-B

BM-A provides the starting elevation, TP-A transfers control between setups, and BM-B provides the closing check.

Engineering output

Longitudinal profile

The reduced elevations are used to produce the final ground profile, design grade, and cut/fill interpretation.

2 / 6 · Concept — Height of Instrument Method

Each elevation must remain traceable to the sight that produced it.

Backsight · BS

Establish the height of instrument

A backsight is taken on a point of known elevation or on the turning point after moving the level.

Intermediate sight · IS

Reduce profile stations

Intermediate sights obtain elevations for the regular centerline stations while the instrument remains in the same setup.

Foresight · FS

Transfer or close control

A foresight is taken on TP-A before moving the instrument and on BM-B to close the run.

Two setups are used in the model: I-01 serves BM-A through TP-A, then I-02 continues from TP-A through the BM-B closing observation.
3 / 6 · Concept — Required Formulas

Reduce the observations, verify closure, then compare ground against grade.

HI = Eknown + BS Example at I-01: 100.000 + 1.400 = 101.400 m.
Epoint = HI − R Use the active height of instrument and the observed rod reading to compute each profile elevation.
e = 98.423 − 98.420 = +0.003 m The computed BM-B is 3 mm high. The simulation compares this with the assigned ±3.980 mm tolerance.
Δ = Eground − Egrade With Eg(x) = 100.000 − 0.012x: positive Δ is cut and negative Δ is fill.
4 / 6 · Step-by-Step — Field Procedure

Run the profile from the starting benchmark to the closing benchmark.

1

Prepare control

Establish the centerline stations, identify BM-A and BM-B, and confirm TP-A at 0+050.

2

Set up I-01

Backsight BM-A with BS = 1.400 m to establish HI₁ = 101.400 m.

3

Observe first profile

Observe 0+000, 0+020, and 0+040, then take the foresight on TP-A.

4

Transfer control

Keep the rod on TP-A, move the instrument to I-02, and take the TP-A backsight to establish HI₂.

5

Complete and close

Observe 0+060, 0+080, and 0+100, then close with the final foresight on BM-B at 0+110.

5 / 6 · Step-by-Step — Reduction & Final Profile

Check the run before revealing the adjusted engineering profile.

1

Check closure

Compare computed BM-B = 98.423 m with known BM-B = 98.420 m. The model misclosure is +0.003 m.

2

Apply the accepted correction

Preserve the raw observations and apply the listed corrections to obtain the adjusted profile elevations.

3

Draw and interpret

Plot the adjusted ground line and the assigned grade line. Read Δ as cut when positive and fill when negative.

The final simulation retains the original field sequence, 3D instrument/rod visualization, closure check, and longitudinal-profile drawing.
