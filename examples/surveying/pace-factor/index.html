FW01 · Fundamentals of Surveying Fieldwork

Pace Factor

Calibrate a repeatable distance-per-pace factor on a measured 50.00 m line, screen the observations only after all trials are complete, and apply the retained factor to an independent unknown line.

Field use

Rapid reconnaissance

Pacing provides approximate horizontal distances when taping or electronic distance measurement is unnecessary or impractical.

Calibration

Known 50.00 m line

Repeated walks on the same measured line establish a personal factor from actual field observations.

Convention

One normal step = one pace

The same counting convention is used in calibration and application so the factor remains internally consistent.

Concept · Calibration

Observe the gait you actually use.

Natural approach

Begin before Point A

Enter the measured segment at a stable walking rhythm. Counting begins at A, not during the acceleration into the course.

Walk through

Finish beyond Point B

The final counted pace lands on B, but the walker continues beyond the endpoint so the last step is not artificially shortened.

Only the footfalls within A–B contribute to the recorded pace count. Approach and walk-through are shown for field realism but are not counted.

Required formulas

Keep every result traceable to raw observations.

PFᵢ = L / NᵢTrial factor from measured line length L and observed pace count Nᵢ.
PF = ΣL / ΣNWorking factor computed directly from the retained line lengths and retained pace counts.
D = PF × NEstimated unknown distance from the calibrated factor and the observed unknown-line pace count.
Field sequence

Collect first. Screen second.

1 · Calibrate

Walk seven trials

Alternate A→B and B→A and preserve every raw pace count before any screening decision.

2 · Screen

Remove one low and one high

This exercise prescribes exclusion of one lowest and one highest pace-count trial, leaving five retained observations.

3 · Apply

Pace the unknown line

Use the same step convention and estimate the unknown distance from the calibrated working factor.
